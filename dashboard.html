The Through-Line

The Consistent Pattern

In every race effort — 2017, 2018, 2023, 2024, London 2026 — you hit a wall between miles 16 and 19. It moves later as fitness improves. London pushed it to 18. Chicago needs to push it past 20. That only happens with higher mileage, longer long runs, and more MP work in training. (Chicago 2025 is excluded — that was a Team 2 fun run, not a race effort.)

FFRT structure is building real speed — the Shamrock Shuffle PR and Soldier Field 10 both show aerobic power is strong. The Tuesday mid-week long run addition is the smart new variable for Chicago. Your half times are competitive; the marathon just needs the muscular endurance to match.

Mobility Menu

What "hip and ankle mobility" or "light mobility / activation" actually means on a given night. Pick 2-3, a few minutes each, floor or standing while the TV's on.

Hip & Ankle Mobility
90/90 hip switches — 8-10 reps each side. Rotates the hips through internal and external range.
World's Greatest Stretch — 5 reps each side. Hits hip flexors, hamstrings, and thoracic spine in one move.
Ankle rocks (knee-to-wall) — 10-15 each side. Drives dorsiflexion, the range you lose that shows up as tight calves and Achilles.
Standing figure-4 stretch — 30-45 sec each side. Opens the glutes and hips after sitting all day.
Leg swings (front-back, side-side) — 10 each direction, holding a wall or counter for balance.
Light Mobility / Activation
Walking lunges with a rotation — 8-10 each leg, light and controlled, not for burn.
Banded lateral walks — 10 steps each direction. Wakes up the glutes without any real fatigue.
Single-leg RDL reach — 5-8 each leg, bodyweight only. Primes hamstrings, glutes, and balance for tomorrow.
Slow calf raises — 15-20 reps. Primes calves and Achilles, useful the night before speed work.
Cat-cow + bird dog — 5-8 reps each. Wakes up the spine and core without any load.

What Actually Matters, In Order

Stretching, Theragun, and NormaTec aren't equally useful. Here's the priority order backed by the evidence, from most to least important on a given night.

1
Mobility and strength work. This is the one with real injury-prevention evidence. Strength training cuts injury rates by roughly half in the research; stretching alone doesn't move the needle at all. A few minutes of hip and ankle mobility most nights beats anything else on this list.
2
NormaTec. Genuine evidence for cutting perceived soreness and restoring how muscles feel, strongest around the 48-hour mark. Best spent on your two hardest nights: Wednesday and Saturday.
3
Theragun. Real but modest evidence for short-term blood flow and soreness relief. Good as a spot treatment on whatever you worked that day. Skip it on your legs the night before a speed session, you don't want them flat.
4
Static stretching. The weakest evidence of the four. A trial of over 2,700 runners found no injury benefit, and it doesn't reduce soreness either. Do it if it helps you wind down and sleep, not because it's "recovery."

None of this beats sleep and protein. These are the extras, not the foundation.
